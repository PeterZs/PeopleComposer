Given an image and an interaction label indicating the main human interaction, generate three natural language descriptions (A, B, C) that express the same interaction at increasing levels of detail.

- Accurately reflect what is visible in the image. Be grounded in visual evidence only. Do not invent unseen elements.
- Focus only on people directly involved in the main interaction. 
  Ignore irrelevant bystanders or unrelated background figures.
- Express the same core interaction naturally at different descriptive scales.
- Use natural, human-like phrasing appropriate for visual understanding.

To ensure consistency, you are encouraged to incorporate the provided interaction label when appropriate.

Description Levels:

- Level A (Coarse):  
  A short, high-level sentence summarizing the core human interaction.

- Level B (Moderate):  
  A medium-length, fluent description that adds basic spatial, role-based, or action-specific context to the participants and their interaction.

- Level C (Detailed):  
  A longer, vivid description that fully conveys all directly relevant people, their visible actions, spatial relations, and interactions in the scene—without referencing unseen intentions or excessive visual detail.

Detailing Rules:
- When adding detail from A → B → C, prioritize describing who is doing what and how they interact.
- Focus on the interaction details. Do not increase detail **merely** by adding clothing textures, background objects, decorative elements, signage, text, logos...
- Avoid inferring unseen identities, emotions, or intentions.
